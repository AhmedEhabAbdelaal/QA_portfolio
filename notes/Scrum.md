## Scrum definition

* A framework that help teams/organizations to generate solutions for complex problems. It is a lightweight framework, theory/philosophy on how to achieve a certain goal and helps the team generate value.Scrum shows the efficiency of the current management, environment & work techniques, so if there is a room for improvement it will be held.


## Scrum theory 

* Scrum is founded on the knowledge and the experience of people (empiricism --> tgrobat) 

## Scrum Pillars 

1. Transparency: The current process/word must be visible to all those who is working on it. without transparency inspection is misleading and wasteful

2. Inspection: the progress toward the goal must be frequently inspect to detect potential problems or changes.Inspection enables adaptation. Inspection without adaptation is considered pointless. Scrum events are
designed to provoke change.

3. Adaptation: an action must be taken when the process or the output is deviated from the wanted goal (this is called adaptation). A self-managing or not empowered people will have hard time in adaptation.


## Scrum values 
* Good use of scrum when the people live with these values:
1. Commitment 
2. Focus 
3. Openness
4. Respect 
5. Courage 

## Scrum Team

* the fundamental unit of scrum is a small team containing:
    - 1 scrum master 
    - 1 product owner 
    - developers

* it's best that a team is a small 10 or less to apply successful communication between them if the team gets larger then multiple scrum team should be considered , they all share the same Product goal,Product Backlog, and Product Owner.


## Developers (Testers included)
* They are the people in the scrum team that are committed to create usable increment in each Sprint

## Product Owner 
* This person communicates clearly the product needs and the requirements in order the scrum team to work on it transparently which result in maximizing the product value also he is accountable for maximizing the product’s value, and manages the Product Backlog (creates items, orders them, keeps them clear).


## Scrum Master 
* this person communicates the guidelines of the Scrum concept across his team members and ensure everyone is maximizing their output whoever the role of this person in the team (product owner or developer)

## Scrum Events 
* Sprint is the process where the idea turned into value, it has a fixed length events of one month or less to keep consistency

* during the sprint, No changes will be executed if it will put the sprint goal in danger, Quality doesn't decrease and a clarified negotiation must take place with the product owner if the scope is changed since there are more things is learned from the Sprint

* Long Sprints are invalid ( since with longer time span between each one may increase complexity or deviation from the goal ), so short span Sprints (month or less) are best to generate more learning cycles and limit risk to waste time and resources 

## Sprint Planning 

* The entire scrum team take part in planning the Sprint where they discuss 3 topics:

    - Topic one(why the sprint is valuable?): the product owner shows how the current sprint will increase the value of the product, the whole team collaborates to define the Sprint goal and why it will increase the value to stakeholders

    - Topic two(What can be done this Sprint?): The developers choose from the Product backlog provided by the product owner the items they will include in this sprint 

    - Topic three(how will the chosen work get done?): For each chosen item from the product backlog the team's quality standard for the increment plan and define the definition of Done for each item. They may break the item into smaller items in order to keep track of a daily progress and server the goal of the run.


## Daily Scrum
- The goal of daily scrum is to show the progress toward the goal and adjust or modify the sprint backlog accordingly to be more efficient, it usually takes around 15 min .

## Sprint review 
- It's the inspection of the outcome of the sprint and a presentation of the results to stakeholders and discussing what to do next accordingly 

## Sprint Retrospective
- Plan ways to increase the quality and effectiveness of the Sprint, Reviewing the past used methods, tools, etc.. in order to show what served the goal and what didn't.


## Scrum Artifacts 
- Each artifact contains and provides the information needed that enhance transparency and focus for the product goal

- Artifacts: 
    - For the product backlog it is the product goal
    - for the Sprint backlog it is the sprint goal
    - for the increment it is the definition of done 

## Product Backlog
- It's an ordered list of what needed to improve the product

## Product goal 
- It's the description of the future state of the product to make a clear view of the target for the scrum team.

## Sprint Backlog 
- the Sprint Backlog is composed of the Sprint Goal (why), the set of Product Backlog items selected for the Sprint (what), as well as an actionable plan for delivering the Increment (how). 

## Sprint goal
- It's a single objective for the Sprint, it shows the exact objective needed to achieve the goal.

## Increment 
- An Increment is a concrete stepping stone toward the Product Goal.An increment is considered an "increment" when it fulfils the definition of Done only but not in the Sprint Review (when the stakeholders review it)

## Definition of Done
- the Definition of Done is a formal description of the state of the Increment when it meets the quality measures required for the product.
